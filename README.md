# Trader Performance vs. Market Sentiment (Hyperliquid)

**Data Science / Analytics Intern — Round-0 Assignment | Primetrade.ai**

Analysis of how Bitcoin market sentiment (Fear/Greed) relates to trader behavior and performance on Hyperliquid, with actionable, sentiment-conditioned trading strategy recommendations.

---

## Project Overview

This project investigates whether market-wide sentiment (as measured by the Bitcoin Fear & Greed Index) is associated with differences in trader **performance** (PnL, win rate, drawdown) and trader **behavior** (leverage, trade frequency, position sizing, long/short bias) on Hyperliquid. The analysis merges 2,644 days of sentiment data with 211,224 individual trade records from 32 accounts, segments traders into behavioral cohorts, and translates the findings into two concrete, data-backed strategy recommendations.

The project is structured in three parts:

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **A — Data Preparation** | Load, inspect, clean, and merge the two raw datasets into a single analysis-ready file. |
| **B — Analysis** | Compute performance and behavior metrics by sentiment, segment traders, and generate all charts/tables. |
| **C — Insights & Strategy** | Written interpretation of the results: key insights, strategy recommendations, and limitations. |

## Dataset Description

### 1. Bitcoin Fear & Greed Index (`fear_greed_index.csv`)
- **2,644 rows**, no missing values, no duplicates
- Columns: `timestamp` (Unix epoch), `value` (0–100 sentiment score), `classification` (`Extreme Fear`, `Fear`, `Neutral`, `Greed`, `Extreme Greed`), `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`)
- Date range: **2018-02-01 to 2025-05-02** (daily granularity)

### 2. Hyperliquid Historical Trader Data (`historical_data.csv`)
- **211,224 rows**, no missing values, no duplicates
- Trade-level records across **32 accounts** and **246 instruments**
- Key columns: `Account`, `Coin`, `Execution Price`, `Size Tokens`, `Size USD`, `Side` (BUY/SELL), `Timestamp IST`, `Start Position`, `Direction`, `Closed PnL`, `Fee`
- Date range: **2023-05-01 to 2025-05-01**

> **Note:** The trade data covers 480 unique trading days, of which 479 have a matching sentiment record (see [Limitations](#future-improvements--known-limitations)). The dataset does **not** include a native leverage/margin column — this is addressed with a derived proxy (see [Analysis Performed](#analysis-performed)).

## Analysis Performed

**Part A — Data Preparation**
- Inspected both datasets for shape, dtypes, missing values, and duplicates.
- Parsed timestamps into `datetime` and derived a common daily `Date` key.
- Left-joined trade records to daily sentiment classification on `Date`.
- Saved the merged, analysis-ready dataset (`merged_data.csv`, 211,224 rows × 20 columns).

**Part B — Analysis**
- **Performance by sentiment:** total/average Closed PnL and win rate (computed on closing trades, i.e. `Closed PnL ≠ 0`), plus a daily cumulative-PnL **drawdown proxy** (cumulative PnL minus running peak) to benchmark risk across regimes.
- **Behavior by sentiment:** trades per day, average trade size (USD), Long/Short (BUY:SELL) ratio, and a bounded **leverage proxy** — `Size USD / (existing position notional + Size USD)` — constructed because the dataset has no native leverage/margin field.
- **Trader segmentation:** all 32 accounts split at the median into **High vs. Low Leverage**, **Frequent vs. Infrequent**, and **Consistent Winner vs. Inconsistent** cohorts, each compared on average total PnL and win rate.
- **Charts:** 7 required comparison charts plus 1 supporting cumulative PnL/drawdown chart, all saved to `charts/`.

**Part C — Insights & Strategy**
- Three key insights derived strictly from the Part B outputs, each tied to a specific chart.
- Two actionable strategy recommendations, each specifying target segment, applicable sentiment regime, recommended action, reasoning, and expected benefit.
- A full list of assumptions and limitations.

## Results

| Sentiment | Total PnL ($) | Win Rate (%) | Avg Trades/Day | Avg Leverage Proxy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Extreme Fear | 739,110 | 76.2 | 1,529 | 0.066 |
| Fear | 3,357,155 | 87.3 | 680 | 0.067 |
| Neutral | 1,292,921 | 82.4 | 562 | 0.087 |
| Greed | 2,150,129 | 76.9 | 261 | 0.106 |
| Extreme Greed | 2,715,171 | 89.2 | 351 | 0.130 |

**Key takeaways** (full detail in `part_c_insights_and_strategy.md`):
1. **Fear regimes are profitable but volatile** — Fear days produced the highest total PnL ($3.36M) but also the deepest average daily drawdown (–$38.1K), while Extreme Greed showed the shallowest drawdown (–$2.7K).
2. **Leverage rises with Greed, but win rate doesn't track it** — average leverage proxy climbs monotonically from Extreme Fear (0.066) to Extreme Greed (0.130), yet win rate dips in Greed (76.9%); High-Leverage accounts earned less on average ($267K) than Low-Leverage accounts ($374K).
3. **Behavior shifts more in style than in size** — Extreme Fear shows 2–6× higher trade frequency without larger trade sizes, while Extreme Greed uniquely shows a sell-skewed Long/Short ratio (0.81), consistent with profit-taking into strength.

All supporting charts are available in the [`charts/`](./charts) folder.

## Strategy Recommendations

1. **Cap exposure for Frequent traders during Extreme Fear.** Extreme Fear combines the highest trade frequency (1,529/day) with the lowest total PnL and among the deepest drawdowns. A soft daily trade-count/exposure cap for the Frequent-trader segment specifically on Extreme Fear days should improve risk-adjusted returns without reducing their more productive Greed-day activity.

2. **Tighten leverage limits for High-Leverage traders during Greed/Extreme Greed.** Leverage rises steadily with sentiment while High-Leverage accounts show lower average total PnL than Low-Leverage accounts. A leverage ceiling that scales down as sentiment moves into Greed should reduce variance and protect capital ahead of a potential sentiment reversal.

Full reasoning and expected benefit for each recommendation are documented in `part_c_insights_and_strategy.md`.

## Future Improvements / Known Limitations

- **Leverage proxy, not true leverage:** the dataset has no native margin/leverage column; the leverage proxy used here reflects *relative* position sizing (0–1 scale), not an actual leverage multiple. Future work with true margin data would allow more precise risk quantification.
- **Six unmatched trade rows:** 6 of 211,224 trade rows (<0.01%) had no matching sentiment date and were excluded from Part B.
- **Realized PnL only:** `Closed PnL` captures realized profit only; unrealized PnL on open positions is not captured, understating true mark-to-market performance at any point in time.
- **Daily-level sentiment granularity:** sentiment is applied uniformly to all trades within a calendar day; intraday sentiment shifts are not modeled. Incorporating hourly sentiment (if available) could sharpen the analysis.
- **Small, static account universe:** only 32 accounts are available, and segments are defined using a single whole-period median rather than a rolling or time-varying threshold. A larger account sample and rolling segmentation would improve robustness.
- **No asset-level breakdown:** all results are aggregated across 246 instruments; a per-instrument analysis (e.g., BTC vs. altcoins) could reveal sentiment effects that differ by asset class.
- **Bonus items not yet implemented:** a predictive model for next-day profitability/volatility, formal behavioral clustering, and a lightweight Streamlit dashboard are natural next steps for extending this analysis.
